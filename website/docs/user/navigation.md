# Navigation

Tap **Start** on a route and the map follows you. The panel at the top shows
the next manoeuvre with its distance and, if there is one, the one after it
(**Then**). At the bottom: the arrival time, the time and distance left, and
**Stop**.

## Voice

The app speaks every instruction twice: well in advance ("In 800 metres, turn
right onto the A28") and just before the turn. Roads, exits and directions
are read from the signs. **Mute** silences the voice; the panel keeps working.

On a phone the voice keeps going with the screen off.

## Lanes

Where OpenStreetMap knows the lanes, the panel shows them before a junction:
the lanes you can take are lit, the others dimmed. The voice tells you how
many of the lanes are right ("two of four lanes").

## Speed

Bottom right you see your speed and the speed limit. The limit comes from
OpenStreetMap, including limits that depend on the time of day (such as 130
at night on Dutch motorways), and, where the server has live traffic, from the
temporary limits and the overhead signs (matrix signs). The lowest one wins.

**Speed cameras**, average speed checks and red light cameras show up ahead
of you with the speed they check; in an average speed check the app shows your
average and the distance left.

## Traffic while driving

With live traffic, the app warns you about an accident, a broken-down vehicle,
an object on the road or an open bridge on your route ("Caution: accident in
2 kilometres"). The overhead signs above the motorway are drawn as they are
shown: a speed, a closed lane or an arrow.

## Rerouting

Miss a turn and the app recalculates from where you are, without asking. When
a faster route comes up along the way, for example because a jam clears, the
app says so and shows it on screen with **Take it** or **Ignore**; it does
not change your route on its own.

## Arrival

At the destination the app says "You have arrived" and shows **Done**. If
the route has stops, it continues to the next one.

## Re-centre

Pan the map during navigation to look around; **Re-centre** puts you back.
You can change the route while driving with **Edit route**; stop the
navigation first when you want a completely different destination.

## Without a car

Navigation works for cycling and walking too, with the same voice and
rerouting; the panel then leaves out lanes and speed limits.
